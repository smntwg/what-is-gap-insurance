GAP insurance for cash buyers

Home / Guides / GAP insurance for cash buyers

If you paid cash, there is no finance company to settle, so finance GAP has nothing to pay. Return to invoice or vehicle replacement can still matter if the insurer's market value would leave you short of the price you paid, or short of another new car. Many cash buyers of older cars reasonably decide the gap is too small to insure.

The shortfall is your own money, not a debt

A write-off does not leave a cash buyer owing a finance company. It leaves them with a market-value cheque that may be less than the price they paid, or less than the cost of a similar replacement. The ABI describes the motor payout as current market value, and describes GAP as the difference between that and the original purchase price. Citizens Advice says you can challenge a market value you think is low. Do that before you assume you need a second policy to fill a hole that a better motor settlement would shrink.

MoneyHelper mentions GAP in the context of owing more than an insurance payout. If you do not owe anyone, that version of the problem does not arise. The version that can remain is depreciation.

A new car bought for cash, then an older one

Example only

£7,750 in month 16, and an £800 gap on an older car

Nora pays £27,500 cash for a new car in York. She is the first keeper. Her comprehensive policy includes new-car replacement for the first 12 months only. In month 7 the car is written off and the motor policy replaces it. A GAP policy would not add a second car on top of that benefit.

In month 16 the same kind of loss is a market-value claim. The insurer offers £19,750. Return to invoice tops up £7,750, back to the £27,500 she paid. She is not in debt. The question is whether she wants to self-fund £7,750. The figures are an illustration.

Idris pays £8,400 cash for an eight-year-old car advertised in line with similar cars. A year later the market value is assessed at £7,600. The gap is £800. He does not buy GAP. For a cash purchase close to market value, that is a reasonable decision, and it is a different decision from Nora's.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

Agreed value can do this job instead

If the car is specialist enough to be on an agreed value motor policy, and the agreed figure is the number you care about, the motor policy may already pay what return to invoice would have topped up. Agreed value is not GAP. The difference is worked through in return to invoice vs vehicle replacement vs agreed value. Keep the agreed figure current. An old agreed value that no longer matches the car is a motor dispute waiting to happen.

How cash buyers get sold the wrong product

Finance GAP sold to someone with nothing to finance is a poor match, even if the premium looks small. The dealer still has to say whether the contract is optional and still has to show the premium on its own, under ICOBS 6A.1. Use the deferral to read which figure the policy actually pays. If you are comparing a used car bought for cash with a financed one, the used car guide puts those two outcomes side by side. Whether the cover is worth the premium at all is the question in is GAP insurance worth it.

Common questions

Is GAP pointless if I have no finance?
Finance GAP is. Cover that puts you back to the invoice, or towards a new equivalent, is a separate decision. It is about depreciation, not about a lender.
I put the car on a credit card. Is that finance GAP?
A credit card bill is not a secured hire purchase or PCP agreement. A finance GAP policy written for those agreements may not pay the card. If you want the purchase price back, look at return to invoice and check that the wording pays you, not a motor lender you do not have.
Do cash buyers get the dealer deferral as well?
Yes, if the dealer is selling GAP in connection with the vehicle. The rule is about that sale. It does not depend on you having borrowed.
Should I rely on savings instead?
If the savings are there for this, and replacing the car would not wipe out your emergency fund, many cash buyers decline GAP. That is a coherent choice, particularly when the car was bought close to market value.
