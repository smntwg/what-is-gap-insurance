GAP insurance when your car is stolen or written off

Home / Guides / GAP insurance when your car is stolen or written off

GAP insurance is built for two outcomes: the motor insurer writes the car off, or it pays an agreed value for a theft where the car is not recovered. It is not a repair fund. The amount it pays is the difference between that motor settlement and the invoice, the finance balance, the lease termination charge, or a replacement cost, depending on the policy you bought.

What counts as a write-off

The ABI says a total loss, also called a write-off, is typically when the repair cost is higher than the vehicle's value. It can also be an economic total loss once other costs, such as a courtesy car, are counted. Insurers use a salvage code. GOV.UK divides insurance total losses into two groups. An actual loss, category A or B, should not be repaired and put back on the road. A constructive loss, category S or N, could be repaired, but the cost of doing so would exceed the replacement value.

If your insurer repairs the car, there is no GAP claim. If they write it off, the offer should be the market value. Citizens Advice says you can dispute that value with prices of similar cars, and that if you keep a repairable salvage car the insurer deducts the salvage value from the payout. Read your GAP wording before you keep the salvage. Some policies use the market value before that deduction. Some use the net amount that was actually paid.

Unrecovered theft is not given a category

The ABI draws a line that matters for the paperwork. A stolen car that is recovered beyond repair can be declared a write-off. A stolen car that is not recovered cannot. The insurer contacts you to agree a valuation and the cost of a replacement. A GAP policy is generally written to follow that theft settlement as well as a write-off, but only the insured events in your schedule count. Do not assume the word write-off in a headline is the only trigger, and do not assume theft is included if the schedule does not say so.

The same shortfall, two sets of paperwork

Example only

£5,400 after a category N write-off, and £5,400 after an unrecovered theft

A car financed on PCP is assessed at a market value of £21,000. The repair estimate is £14,500, and once recovery and a courtesy car are included the insurer treats it as a total loss. It is recorded as category N. The finance settlement is £26,400. Finance GAP, if this total loss is covered and the claim limit allows, is claimed for £5,400. If the insurer had repaired the car, the GAP payment would have been zero and the PCP would have carried on.

The same figures as an unrecovered theft: the insurer agrees £21,000, there is no salvage category, and the settlement figure is still £26,400. The shortfall is still £5,400. The GAP file holds a theft settlement letter and a crime reference instead of a category letter. How to assemble that file is in how to make a GAP claim.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

Finance and lease bills do not pause themselves

While the motor and GAP claims are open, the finance or lease agreement is still a contract. MoneyHelper explains early settlement on hire purchase, and the cost of ending a lease early. Tell the lender you have a total loss and ask them not to treat a short delay as arrears if you are waiting for the insurer. Arrears that do build are often excluded from the GAP claim. On a PCP, remember the settlement is likely to include the balloon. That structure is in the PCP guide.

Comprehensive insurance is what produces the £21,000 in the example. GAP only adds the £5,400. The split between those jobs is in GAP vs comprehensive insurance.

Common questions

The insurer wants to repair my car. Can I insist on a write-off so GAP pays?
No. GAP follows a total loss the motor insurer has decided. A repair, even an expensive one, is not a GAP claim. Asking for a write-off the repair cost does not support will not create a shortfall payment.
The other driver was at fault and their insurer is paying. Can I still claim GAP?
Sometimes. It depends whether your GAP wording accepts a market-value settlement from a third party insurer, or whether it expects the claim to go through your own comprehensive policy. Check before you choose a route that skips your own insurer.
What if the car is found after I have been paid for the theft?
Tell both insurers straight away. You cannot keep a full theft payout and the car. They will set out whether the car is returned and the payout is revised, or whether the insurer keeps the car.
Does a category S or N history matter next time?
It can. GOV.UK says a vehicle that was previously a total loss can cost more to insure, and that you should tell the insurer before you buy. Failing to tell them can give them a reason to refuse a later claim. That is a motor insurance issue on the next car, not a second GAP payout.

Related guides

How to make a GAP insurance claim

The motor insurer settles first. Then you claim the shortfall the GAP wording actually describes.

GAP insurance vs comprehensive insurance

Comprehensive pays market value. GAP can top that up. It cannot replace the motor policy.

What GAP insurance does not cover

Repairs, refused motor claims, arrears and sums above the claim limit are the usual gaps in the cover.

GAP insurance for PCP

On a PCP the settlement figure often still includes the balloon. That is the gap finance cover is aimed at.
