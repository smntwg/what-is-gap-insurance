How to make a GAP insurance claim

Home / Guides / How to make a GAP insurance claim

A GAP claim starts only after your motor insurer has agreed a total loss, or agreed a value for an unrecovered theft. You then get the finance or lease figure in writing, and you ask the GAP insurer to pay the difference described in your policy, up to the claim limit. There is no separate emergency payout that arrives before the motor settlement.

Do these things in this order

  1. Deal with safety first. If the car was stolen, report it to the police and keep the crime reference number.
  2. Claim on the comprehensive motor policy, and tell them about any finance or lease. The insurer's offer on a write-off should be market value. Citizens Advice says you can challenge a low offer with prices of similar cars, or with a valuation from an independent engineer if you choose to pay for one.
  3. Read the GAP conditions before you accept a motor offer you think is too low. Some policies let the GAP insurer negotiate the motor value. Some calculate your shortfall from the offer you accept. Accepting a low figure can shrink the GAP claim, or it can be what the wording tells you to do. Know which.
  4. Ask the lender or leasing company for a settlement figure or early termination figure in writing, close to the date of the motor offer. MoneyHelper explains that an HP settlement is not simply the payments you have left.
  5. Tell the GAP insurer inside the time limit printed in the policy. There is no single statutory number of days. Missing the policy limit is a common reason for a dispute, so diary it.
  6. Send what they ask for: the motor settlement letter, the motor schedule, the purchase invoice or hire agreement, the finance agreement, the V5C, the crime reference if it was stolen, and photographs if they want them.
  7. Check the payment goes to the right place, and keep paying the finance until the lender confirms the account is clear.

A worked calculation, including the excess

Example only

£3,750 shortfall, and a policy that contributes £250 towards the excess

A motor insurer offers £19,400 for a written-off family car in Sheffield. That offer is after a £450 policy excess, so the market value they assessed was £19,850 and the customer is treated as standing £450 of it. The hire purchase settlement figure is £23,150.

Settlement £23,150 minus the £19,400 actually paid to the lender is £3,750. The GAP policy in this illustration contributes up to £250 towards the motor excess. The claim is £4,000, if the claim limit allows and the excess clause works on these facts. If the policy does not mention the excess, the claim is £3,750 and the £450 stays with the customer.

If £900 of the settlement is arrears, and arrears are excluded, the eligible claim falls by £900. Exclusions of that sort are listed in what GAP insurance does not cover.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

Write-off and unrecovered theft are not the same paperwork

The ABI says a stolen vehicle that is recovered beyond repair can be a write-off, but a vehicle that is not recovered is not declared a write-off. The insurer agrees a valuation instead. Your GAP claim can follow either settlement if theft and total loss are both insured events in the policy. The categories, and when a repair means there is no GAP claim at all, are in the stolen or written-off guide.

If the offer looks wrong

Ask both insurers for the arithmetic in writing: market value, excess, salvage deduction if you kept the car, finance figure, excluded negative equity, and claim limit. Then complain to the firm whose figure you dispute. The Financial Ombudsman Service will want the firm's final response, or for eight weeks to have passed. You do not need a claims company to do that.

Common questions

Can I claim on GAP if the motor insurer refuses the claim?
Almost never. GAP tops up a motor settlement. If the motor claim is declined, there is no market-value payout to top up. Fixing the motor dispute, or complaining about it, comes before a GAP payment.
How long does a GAP claim take?
The GAP insurer usually waits for the motor settlement letter and the finance figure. There is no FCA rule that a GAP claim must be paid within a set number of days. Keep the finance company informed so arrears do not build while the two insurers write to each other.
Who is paid?
The finance or lease company is commonly paid first. Anything above what you owe is paid as the policy sets out, often to you. Get the split in writing so you do not spend money that was never coming to your account.
What if I disagree with the GAP offer?
Complain to the GAP insurer first and ask for the calculation. If you are still unhappy after a final response, or after eight weeks, you can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service. The same path is set out on the mis-selling page, and it applies to claim disputes as well as sales disputes.

Related guides

GAP insurance when your car is stolen or written off

GAP follows a total loss or an unrecovered theft settlement. A repair is not a claim.

What GAP insurance does not cover

Repairs, refused motor claims, arrears and sums above the claim limit are the usual gaps in the cover.

Cancelling GAP insurance and refunds

Most policies can be cancelled. The 14-day right and a later pro-rata refund are not the same thing.

GAP insurance vs comprehensive insurance

Comprehensive pays market value. GAP can top that up. It cannot replace the motor policy.
