GAP insurance for leased cars

Home / Guides / GAP insurance for leased cars

On contract hire, also called personal contract hire, you never own the car. If it is written off or stolen, the leasing company is the owner who receives the motor insurance money, and the contract will usually still charge you to end the hire early. Lease GAP is aimed at the difference between that insurance money and the early termination charge.

What contract hire leaves you owing

MoneyHelper's personal contract hire guide is plain about the shape of the deal. You agree a term and a mileage limit, you pay an upfront fee and monthly rentals, and you normally have no option to buy the car. You insure it, fuel it and service it. You cannot simply give it back early without a large fee. MoneyHelper's page on ending car finance early makes the same point for personal contract hire: getting out early is expensive because the contract says so.

A total loss is an early end you did not choose. The motor insurer, under a comprehensive or fire-and-theft settlement, pays the owner the market value. The ABI describes that market-value payout. The leasing company then applies its early termination clause. Your exposure is whatever is left after the insurance money is credited. That remainder is what contract hire GAP is written to cover.

A worked contract hire example

Example only

A £4,690 bill after a total loss in month 9

Helen hires a car on a 36-month personal contract hire. The monthly rental is £329 and she paid an initial rental of £1,974. In month 9 the car is a total loss after a collision in Glasgow. The leasing company's early termination charge is £16,840. The motor insurer pays the leasing company the market value, £12,150.

Helen is billed for £4,690. A contract hire GAP policy that covers the early termination shortfall is claimed for £4,690, if that is inside the claim limit and the policy names her as the person liable under the hire agreement.

The policy in this illustration does not give her a deposit for the next car. It does not pay the £180 a month she is later billed for a scuffed alloy that the leasing company treats as beyond fair wear, and it does not pay an excess-mileage charge. Those are hire-contract charges. They are listed among the things GAP usually does not cover.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

Names, VAT and vans

The person or business that signed the hire contract should match the person the GAP policy will pay on behalf of. A policy in a driver's name does not automatically cover a limited company's lease. If the business is VAT registered, ask whether the termination figure in a claim is the net amount or includes VAT, and use that same figure on the proposal.

Van contract hire is the same structure with a few extra eligibility questions. Those sit in the van guide. A PCP, where there is an option to own and a balloon, is a different agreement and a different shortfall. Use the PCP guide if the paperwork says personal contract purchase rather than contract hire.

What to ask before you buy lease GAP

  • Does the wording pay the early termination shortfall, or does it pay a return to invoice on a car you do not own?
  • Are excess mileage, damage charges and arrears excluded?
  • Is the claim limit at least as large as a pessimistic termination shortfall in the first year?
  • Does the motor policy have to be comprehensive, and is business use correct if this is a company car?
  • Who is paid: you, or the leasing company direct?

Common questions

I do not own the car. Can I insure the gap?
Yes, if the policy is written for contract hire or lease. You are insuring your liability to the owner under the hire contract, not a car you own. A return-to-invoice policy aimed at a purchase you never made is the wrong product.
Does the leasing company's insurance replace GAP?
You are usually required to insure the car on a comprehensive policy yourself. That policy pays market value to the owner. It does not automatically pay the early termination charge in the hire contract. Read both documents.
What if the insurance payout is higher than the termination charge?
There may be no shortfall for you to pay. Any extra is dealt with under the lease and the motor policy. GAP does not pay a windfall on top of a lease that has already been cleared.
Are wear and tear or excess mileage covered?
Usually not. Those are charges under the hire contract for condition and miles, not a depreciation gap. A policy that says it covers the early termination shortfall still needs to be read for exclusions on mileage, damage and arrears.

GAP insurance for PCP

On a PCP the settlement figure often still includes the balloon. That is the gap finance cover is aimed at.

GAP insurance for vans

A financed or hired van can leave a gap. Check vans are eligible, the use is insured, and which VAT figure is used.

GAP insurance when your car is stolen or written off

GAP follows a total loss or an unrecovered theft settlement. A repair is not a claim.

What GAP insurance does not cover

Repairs, refused motor claims, arrears and sums above the claim limit are the usual gaps in the cover.
