Cancelling GAP insurance and refunds

Home / Guides / Cancelling GAP insurance and refunds

You can cancel GAP insurance. For most insurance contracts the FCA gives you 14 days to cancel without penalty and without giving a reason. After that, any refund is whatever the policy says. A claim that has already been paid will usually stop a refund. Cancelling GAP does not cancel the car finance, the lease, or the motor policy.

The 14-day right

ICOBS 7.1 gives a consumer the right to cancel, without penalty and without giving any reason, within 14 days for a general insurance contract or distance contract. The period starts on the day the contract is concluded, or on the day you receive the contractual terms and the required pre-contract information if that is later. There are exceptions. One of them is a general insurance contract that is not a distance contract and is sold by an intermediary who is an unauthorised person, other than an appointed representative. A showroom sale through an authorised firm or its appointed representative is not in that exception. A policy schedule should say how to cancel. Read that wording, because it has to reflect the right that applies to your sale.

If you cancel inside the statutory period, ICOBS 7.2 says you can be required to pay for the service already provided, in proportion to the full cover, and the amount must not be a penalty. The firm can only charge an amount it told you about. Many GAP policies go further than the minimum and refund the whole premium if you cancel in the cooling-off period and you have not claimed. That full refund is a term of those policies, not a second FCA rule.

Two refund illustrations

Example only

A full cooling-off refund, then a later pro-rata refund

The premium is £312, paid in one go. You cancel on day 6. You have not claimed. The policy says the cooling-off refund is the full premium. The refund is £312.

A different policy costs £360 for 36 months. You cancel after 12 months, with no claim. The terms say the refund is the unused premium on a pro-rata basis, minus a £20 administration fee that was printed in the terms you received at the start. The unused portion is £240. After the £20 fee the refund is £220.

The £20 is an illustration of a fee the contract disclosed. It is not a standard charge across the market. Inside the 14-day statutory window, a fee that worked as a penalty would sit badly with ICOBS 7.2. After that window, the contract you agreed is what applies.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

What does not cancel the policy for you

  • Paying off the PCP or HP early. Tell the GAP insurer. You may be due a refund, and you may no longer have a finance shortfall to insure.
  • Handing a contract hire car back at the scheduled end. The policy does not always end on the hire end date unless the term was matched and the insurer is told.
  • Cancelling the comprehensive motor policy. GAP is a separate contract. Without a motor settlement it may be useless, but the premium can continue until you cancel it.
  • A claim that was paid. Expect the terms to say there is no premium refund after a claim. Ask if you are mid-claim and thinking of cancelling. Do not cancel first and hope.

When cancellation is the wrong tool

If the policy was added without a proper choice, the premium was not shown separately, or the dealer completed the sale without the deferral, you may be looking at a complaint rather than a routine cancellation. How to do that, including the eight weeks the firm generally has, is in GAP insurance mis-selling. The information the dealer should have given you is in the FCA rules guide. The Financial Ombudsman Service is free if the firm's final response is not good enough.

Common questions

Can the dealer refuse a cancellation I make within 14 days?
If the statutory right applies, no. Put the cancellation in writing and keep proof of when you sent it. Under ICOBS 7.1, a notice on paper or another durable medium counts if it is dispatched before the deadline, even if the firm reads it afterwards.
I pay monthly. Do I get the months I have not used?
That depends on whether you have one premium spread over the term, or a contract that renews. Ask for the cancellation sum in writing and compare it with the terms you were given at the start.
I have sold the car. Does GAP cancel itself?
Not always. Tell the GAP insurer. If you no longer have an insurable interest in the shortfall, you may not need the policy, but the premium does not stop until the insurer confirms the cancellation and any refund.
I think the dealer was too expensive. Is that a refund?
Inside the cancellation period, cancel if you do not want the policy. Outside it, regretting the price is not automatically a refund. A sale that broke the information or deferral rules is a complaint, which is a different route, set out in the mis-selling guide.
