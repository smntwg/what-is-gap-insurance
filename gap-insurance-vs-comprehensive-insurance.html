GAP insurance vs comprehensive insurance

Home / Guides / GAP insurance vs comprehensive insurance

Comprehensive motor insurance pays for your own car if it is stolen or written off, usually at market value. GAP insurance does not replace that policy and you cannot drive on GAP alone. It can pay an extra amount after the motor insurer has paid, and only for the shortfall its wording describes. If your motor policy already replaces a new car, GAP may be duplicating a benefit you have for that period.

What comprehensive insurance actually pays

The ABI describes comprehensive cover as the highest level of motor insurance: injury and property damage you cause, and your own vehicle if it is damaged, stolen or destroyed by fire. For a total loss it says the insurer should cover replacement at current market value if you have comprehensive cover, or third party fire and theft for the events that policy includes. It also says GAP cover, where you have it, pays the difference between the original purchase price and current market value. That sentence matches return to invoice. It does not describe every GAP wording. Finance GAP uses the amount you owe. Contract hire GAP uses the early termination charge.

Citizens Advice says a write-off offer should be the market value at the time, and that you can dispute it with evidence of similar cars for sale. The motor excess is still yours unless a benefit says otherwise. Comprehensive does not mean every loss is paid in full.

Where the two policies meet

The motor insurer decides the insured value and pays it. The GAP insurer looks at the gap between that payment and a second figure in its wording. Agreed value or a new-car replacement benefit changes the motor payment, which changes or removes the gap. Those three figures are compared on one car in the return to invoice, replacement and agreed value guide.

New-car replacement on a comprehensive policy, where it exists, can make vehicle replacement GAP unnecessary for that limited period. It does nothing for a used car, and it ends when the benefit period ends. After that you are back to market value, which is when a GAP policy bought for three years may start doing work the motor policy no longer does.

One settlement, split between the two policies

Example only

Market value £16,500, invoice £23,000, HP settlement £18,200

A comprehensive insurer assesses a written-off car in Brighton at £16,500 and pays that sum, before thinking about the £400 excess, which the policy collects from the customer separately. The purchase invoice was £23,000. The hire purchase settlement is £18,200.

Comprehensive pays the £16,500. It does not pay the £6,500 fall from the invoice. Finance GAP, topping up to the settlement, pays £1,700 if £16,500 has gone to the lender. Return to invoice pays £6,500 if it tops up to £23,000. The customer has one GAP policy, so they receive the motor payout plus one of those top-ups, not both.

If a new-car replacement benefit had applied, the motor insurer would have been dealing with a new car rather than £16,500, and the GAP arithmetic would have been done again from that better motor outcome. It is not added on top of a market-value cheque as well.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

What comprehensive will not hand to GAP

If the motor claim is refused, GAP has nothing to top up. If the car is repaired, GAP has nothing to top up. If you keep the salvage, the motor payout is reduced by the salvage value, and the GAP wording decides whether it uses the gross value or the net payment. GOV.UK explains which written-off categories can go back on the road. The practical claim consequences are in when your car is stolen or written off, and the exclusion list is in what GAP does not cover.

Common questions

Can I drive with only GAP insurance?
No. Motor insurance that covers liability to other people is compulsory. GAP is optional and sits on top of a motor policy. The police and the Motor Insurance Database are interested in the motor policy, not the GAP policy.
Does fully comprehensive mean new for old for the whole term?
No. New-car replacement, where a policy has it, is a specific benefit with a time limit and conditions. It is often the first 12 months, for the first registered keeper, under a mileage cap. It is not a promise that every comprehensive policy replaces the car like for like for three years.
I have third party, fire and theft. Is that enough for GAP?
The ABI says fire and theft cover can pay market value for those events. Many GAP policies still insist the vehicle is insured comprehensively. An at-fault accident is not covered for your own car on third party, fire and theft, so there is no accident payout for GAP to top up. Check the GAP eligibility line before you rely on it.
Will a GAP claim affect my no claims discount?
The motor claim is what usually affects a no claims discount. GAP is a separate contract. Do not assume a GAP claim is invisible to the motor insurer, and do not assume it removes a discount the motor claim has already affected. Read both wordings.
