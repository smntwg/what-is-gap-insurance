Can I buy GAP insurance after buying the car?

Home / Guides / Can I buy GAP insurance after buying the car?

Yes. You can usually buy GAP insurance after you have taken the car, but only inside the time limit set by that provider, and only before a loss. There is no UK law that says you must buy it on the day, and there is no single number of days that every provider uses. The FCA deferral slows a dealer sale down. It is not a deadline to buy from someone else.

The dealer wait is not your deadline

If a firm sells GAP in connection with the vehicle, ICOBS 6A.1 says it cannot conclude that contract until at least two clear days after it gave you the prescribed information, unless you yourself ask to go ahead from the next day and confirm you understand the usual restriction. The FCA introduced that pause, from 1 September 2015, so you could decide whether you needed the product and shop elsewhere. Its 2018 evaluation found that some people, given the time, decided not to buy at all.

A standalone insurer you contact the following week is a different sale. The two-clear-day rule is not a ban on that insurer offering you a policy, and it is not a promise that they will. Their own acceptance rules apply. The detail of the dealer rule is in the FCA rules guide, and the price reason for using the pause is in dealer vs online.

Each provider sets its own window

Some providers will quote only if you bought the car within the last few weeks. Others will still quote a few months after delivery. You will see numbers such as 30, 90 or 180 days in individual policy conditions. None of those is a legal limit, and this site is not going to pretend there is a standard window. Read the assumption on the quote. If the car is already outside it, another provider may still quote, or nobody will.

You will also be asked to confirm that the car has not already been in an incident you might claim for. Cover is not backdated. That is ordinary insurance practice, not a special GAP penalty.

Two timings

Example only

A purchase nine days later, and a request 14 months later

James collects a car in Cardiff on 2 March. The dealer gives him the GAP information that day and offers to add £540 to the invoice. He says no. On 11 March he buys a standalone policy with a £20,000 claim limit for £250. Both prices are an illustration, not a price survey. The sequence is a normal use of the deferral: he heard the offer, he waited, he bought elsewhere.

Ruth bought her car 14 months ago. After a car-park scrape she asks for GAP, hoping to cover a future write-off and, quietly, the scratch. The scratch is already outside any new policy. Many providers will also decline because their purchase window closed months ago. She can ask. She cannot assume either that the window is still open or that the scrape becomes a claim.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

What to have ready

  • The date you took delivery, which may differ from the date you ordered the car.
  • The invoice or hire agreement, and whether any extras were included in the price you want insured.
  • A comprehensive motor policy in force. GAP providers commonly refuse to start cover if you only have third party.
  • The finance or lease settlement basis, so you buy the right type rather than the first name you recognise.
  • Honesty about modifications, a previous write-off category, and existing damage. A wrong answer here is a claims problem later, not a shortcut.

If you buy and then change your mind, the cancellation rules are separate. They are covered in cancelling GAP insurance and refunds.

Common questions

Will my finance be cancelled if I do not buy the dealer's GAP?
It should not. GAP sold with a car has to be described as optional or compulsory. If it is a condition of the finance, that should be in the credit agreement, not only in a sentence at the desk. Ask for the premium as its own figure either way.
Can I add GAP after a write-off has already been declared?
No. Policies are written on the basis that the vehicle has not already been written off, stolen or damaged in the incident you want covered. Buying after a loss is not a claim.
Does the cooling-off period let me buy, claim and cancel?
No. A cancellation right and a claim for a known loss are different things. Insurers will refuse a claim for an incident that happened before the policy started, and a cancellation does not create cover for that incident.
What documents will a later purchase need?
Expect the purchase invoice or hire agreement, proof of the vehicle such as the V5C, the date you took delivery, and details of a comprehensive motor policy. A provider can also ask about mileage and any existing damage.
