GAP insurance for hire purchase

Home / Guides / GAP insurance for hire purchase

Hire purchase GAP covers the difference between the motor insurer's payout and what you still owe the HP lender. A standard HP agreement has no balloon, so the shortfall is often smaller than on a PCP for the same car. It is usually largest in the first year or two, while the balance is still high and the car has already fallen in value.

How HP builds the balance

MoneyHelper describes hire purchase as a deposit plus monthly payments that cover the rest of the car. You normally own it once you have made the final payment. There is no optional balloon. Monthly payments are usually higher than a PCP on the same car because you are paying the whole balance off, not stopping short of a large final sum.

If the car is written off, the lender still wants the early settlement figure. That figure is not 'monthly payment times months left'. Interest that has not yet been earned is rebated. MoneyHelper's guide to ending car finance early explains that early settlement charges on hire purchase are capped by the Consumer Credit Act, and sets out how the cap is worked out. The number you use for a GAP claim is the settlement figure the lender issues, not a guess.

A worked HP example

Example only

A £1,450 shortfall on a three-year-old estate

Tom buys a three-year-old estate in Bristol on hire purchase. The cash price is £16,800. He puts down £1,500, so the amount of credit is £15,300 over 48 months. Eleven months later the car is written off. The motor insurer pays a market value of £12,600 to the lender.

The settlement figure, after the interest rebate, is £14,050. The shortfall is £1,450. In this illustration the gap is real but modest. It is a stronger reason to insure if Tom has no savings than if he has a few thousand pounds set aside.

The same car on a PCP with a £6,000 balloon could show a much higher settlement in month 11. That is why a PCP example and an HP example should not be swapped. See GAP for PCP for the balloon version.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

The half rule does not replace a write-off claim

MoneyHelper explains that you can return an HP car early once you have paid at least half of the total cost, or pay the difference up to that half. If you have already paid more than half, you do not get the extra back. That right is about handing the car back while you still have it. A total loss means the car is going to the insurer, not back to the dealer under a voluntary termination. Do not treat the half rule as a substitute for GAP, and do not cancel a claim because you remember the half rule from the showroom.

Used cars on HP

HP is common on used cars, where depreciation is often slower than on a new car but the balance can still sit above market value early on. Age and mileage limits on the GAP policy matter more here than they do on a new car. The used car guide splits a case where the gap is worth measuring from a case where it is a few hundred pounds.

When you claim, the order is motor settlement, then the lender's figure, then the GAP insurer. That sequence is set out in how to make a GAP claim.

Common questions

I will own the car at the end. Do I still need GAP?
Only while you could owe more than the insured value. Later in the agreement the balance often drops below market value. At that point there is no finance shortfall for a policy to pay.
Does GAP clear the agreement if I miss payments?
No. Arrears, missed instalments and late fees are commonly excluded. GAP is not a payment holiday and it is not a settlement of a healthy agreement.
Is HP GAP different from finance GAP?
Finance GAP is the usual name for a policy that pays up to the outstanding finance. Hire purchase is one of the agreements it is used for. The label on the brochure matters less than a wording that names hire purchase and pays the settlement figure.
What if the motor payout is higher than the settlement figure?
There is no gap. Any surplus after the lender is paid belongs to you, subject to the motor excess and the motor policy. A GAP policy does not pay a further sum just because you have the policy.
