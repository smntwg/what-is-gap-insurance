What is GAP insurance?

A straightforward guide to Guaranteed Asset Protection: what it covers, when you need it, how much it costs, and whether it is worth buying in the UK.

Quick answer

GAP (Guaranteed Asset Protection) insurance is an optional policy that tops up your motor insurer's payout if your car is written off or stolen and not recovered. Your motor insurer pays the car's market value at the time of the loss. GAP pays some or all of the difference between that and what you paid for the car, what you still owe on finance, or what a replacement costs, depending on the type of GAP, up to the claim limit on the policy.

GAP insurance explained in simple terms

When you buy a new or nearly-new car, it starts losing value the moment you drive it away. If the car is written off or stolen six months later, your insurer pays out the current market value, not what you paid for it.

That's where a gap appears. You owe the finance company what you originally borrowed, but the insurance payout is now much less. GAP insurance covers that shortfall so you are not left out of pocket.

An example

You buy a car for £25,000 on finance. A year later it's stolen. Your insurer values it at £19,000 and pays you that. But you still owe the finance company £22,000. That is a £3,000 shortfall, and GAP insurance would cover it. These figures are an illustration, not a real claim.

How does GAP insurance work in the UK?

GAP never pays on its own. It sits behind your comprehensive motor policy and only starts once that claim is settled.

  1. The car is a total loss. It is written off after an accident, fire or flood, or stolen and not recovered, and your motor insurer accepts the claim.
  2. The motor insurer pays market value. The Association of British Insurers explains that a written-off car is settled at its market value at the time of the loss, not the price you paid.
  3. You claim the shortfall from the GAP insurer. You send the motor settlement details, plus the invoice or the finance settlement figure. The GAP policy pays the difference its wording describes, up to the claim limit on your schedule.

If the car is repaired rather than written off, there is nothing for GAP to pay. The steps and paperwork are in how to make a GAP insurance claim and GAP when your car is stolen or written off.

The main types of GAP insurance

Not all GAP insurance is the same. In the UK there are four common types, and picking the right one depends on how you bought the car and when you buy the cover.

Return to Invoice

Pays the difference between your insurer's valuation and the price you paid for the car. Best for outright purchases.

Finance GAP

Pays the difference between your insurer's valuation and what you still owe on finance. Best for PCP or HP agreements.

Vehicle Replacement

Pays towards the cost of a new equivalent, which can be higher than the original invoice if prices have risen.

Return to Value

Pays the difference between the settlement and the car's value when the GAP cover started, as in this published policy wording. Often used when you buy GAP some time after the car.

Agreed value is different again: it is a figure on your motor policy, not a third GAP product. Return to invoice, vehicle replacement and agreed value are compared on one set of numbers.

Is GAP insurance worth it?

Whether GAP insurance is worth buying depends entirely on your situation. It's a useful safety net for some drivers and a waste of money for others. Here's an honest breakdown. The longer version, with a worked example, is in is GAP insurance worth it.

When it's worth it

  • You bought a new or nearly-new car
  • You're on a PCP or HP finance agreement
  • You put down a small or no deposit
  • You can't afford a £2,000 to £5,000 shortfall
  • Your car depreciates quickly (most do)

When it's not worth it

  • You paid cash for a used car
  • Your car is already 3+ years old
  • You have savings to cover any shortfall
  • The premium is more than the likely gap
  • You drive very few miles per year

How much does GAP insurance cost in the UK?

What you pay depends on the car, the claim limit and the length of the policy. A single "typical" price would not be reliable. The FCA's competition work found that GAP sold with the car could be twice as expensive as broadly equivalent cover bought separately. In 2024 the FCA also said that only 6% of the amount customers paid in GAP premiums was paid out in claims. That is why it pays to compare, rather than take the first price in the showroom. Published prices and a rough calculator are in how much is GAP insurance. The detail is in FCA rules on dealer GAP and dealer vs online GAP insurance.

GAP insurance when buying a car

You do not have to buy GAP insurance on the day you get the car. If a dealer, or a firm connected to the sale such as the finance broker, sells GAP with the vehicle, FCA rule ICOBS 6A.1 applies. Before the sale the firm must give you, in writing, the total GAP premium shown separately from other prices, the main features and exclusions, how long the policy lasts, whether it is optional, the fact that GAP is also sold by other firms, and when the sale can be concluded.

The firm then cannot conclude the GAP sale until at least two clear days have passed. If you are given the information on a Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday are the clear days, so Thursday is the earliest sale. The one exception is the day after: you can buy sooner only if you start the purchase yourself and confirm you understand the normal wait.

Other providers set their own limit on how long after the purchase they will still offer a policy. There is no single legal number of days.

Use that pause to compare a policy that is not sold in the same appointment as the car. Buying GAP after you have the car, dealer vs online cover, and the FCA deferral rules explain how the timing works.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need GAP insurance by law?
No. GAP insurance is completely optional. It's a useful safety net but not legally required to drive in the UK.
Does GAP insurance replace my regular car insurance?
No. It sits on top of your comprehensive car insurance. You must have comprehensive cover for GAP insurance to pay out. See GAP insurance vs comprehensive insurance.
Can I cancel GAP insurance?
Yes. For most GAP contracts you have 14 days to cancel without penalty and without giving a reason. After that, any refund is set by the policy. The cancelling guide explains both.
Is GAP insurance worth it on a used car?
It can be, but less often than on a new car. Used cars usually fall in value more slowly, so the gap between market value and what you paid is often smaller. Finance can still leave a shortfall. See GAP insurance for used cars.
Will GAP insurance cover a write-off from any cause?
It follows a motor claim the insurer has agreed to pay, for a total loss or an unrecovered theft. It does not pay for repairs. Accidents, theft, fire and flood can all qualify if your motor insurer accepts the claim. See stolen or written off.
Who regulates GAP insurance, and where can I complain?
GAP is an insurance product regulated by the FCA, so the firm selling it should be on the FCA register. If something goes wrong, complain to the firm first. For most complaints it has up to 8 weeks to send a final response. If you are unhappy with that response, or 8 weeks pass without one, you can take the complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service, which is free. You need to do that within 6 months of the final response. See GAP insurance mis-selling.

The bottom line

GAP insurance can cover a large shortfall if your car is written off or stolen in the first few years, especially if you bought it on finance. It is not always worth the money, and a policy sold with the car can cost more than a separate one.

If a dealer is selling the policy with the car, do not complete that sale in the same conversation. Take the information away, compare it, and decide. The guides below cover finance, leases, claims, cancelling, and what the policy will not pay.

Dealer vs online GAP insurance

The same shortfall can be insured in the showroom or later online. The sale, and often the price, is different.

Return to invoice vs vehicle replacement vs agreed value

Two are types of GAP. Agreed value is a motor policy figure. They do not all pay at once.

GAP insurance for PCP

On a PCP the settlement figure often still includes the balloon. That is the gap finance cover is aimed at.

GAP insurance for hire purchase

HP has no balloon. The shortfall is usually smaller than PCP, and largest early in the agreement.

GAP insurance for leased cars

On contract hire you never own the car. The gap is the early termination charge minus the insurance money.

GAP insurance for used cars

A used car can still leave a gap if you overpaid or you owe more than the insurer will pay.

Can I buy GAP insurance after buying the car?

Usually yes, inside that provider's time limit, and only before a loss. There is no single legal deadline.

How to make a GAP insurance claim

The motor insurer settles first. Then you claim the shortfall the GAP wording actually describes.

Cancelling GAP insurance and refunds

Most policies can be cancelled. The 14-day right and a later pro-rata refund are not the same thing.

What GAP insurance does not cover

Repairs, refused motor claims, arrears and sums above the claim limit are the usual gaps in the cover.

GAP insurance vs comprehensive insurance

Comprehensive pays market value. GAP can top that up. It cannot replace the motor policy.

FCA rules on dealer GAP insurance

Dealers must give you set information and wait. Fair value rules are why sales were paused in 2024.

GAP insurance for cash buyers

No finance means finance GAP has nothing to pay. Invoice or replacement cover is a separate question.

GAP insurance for vans

A financed or hired van can leave a gap. Check vans are eligible, the use is insured, and which VAT figure is used.

GAP insurance for motorbikes

The shortfall works like a car. Fewer policies cover bikes, and agreed value is more common.

GAP insurance when your car is stolen or written off

GAP follows a total loss or an unrecovered theft settlement. A repair is not a claim.

GAP insurance mis-selling

A sale that skips the FCA information rules is a complaint. A price you later regret is not automatically the same thing.
