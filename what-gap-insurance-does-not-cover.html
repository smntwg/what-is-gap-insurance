What GAP insurance does not cover

Home / Guides / What GAP insurance does not cover

GAP insurance pays a defined shortfall after a motor insurer has settled a total loss or an unrecovered theft. It does not pay for repairs. It does not pay when the motor claim is turned down. It does not clear parts of a finance balance the policy has excluded, and it never pays more than its claim limit.

The motor claim has to succeed first

The ABI is clear that a motor policy does not usually pay for an unauthorised driver, use the policy does not allow, an unroadworthy vehicle, wear and tear, or mechanical breakdown. If the motor insurer refuses the claim for one of those reasons, there is no settlement for GAP to top up. Drink or drug driving, undeclared modifications, and using a private car for hire and reward sit in the same place: they are motor policy problems first.

Third party cover does not pay for your own car at all. A GAP policy that requires a comprehensive settlement will not fill that hole. The difference between the two products is set out in GAP vs comprehensive insurance.

What a typical wording leaves out

  • Repairs. If the insurer fixes the car, GAP pays nothing, even if the repair is expensive.
  • Any loss that happened before the GAP policy started.
  • Amounts above the claim limit.
  • Arrears, missed payments and late fees on the finance or lease.
  • Negative equity rolled into the agreement from a previous car, where the wording excludes it.
  • Extras fitted after you bought the policy, unless you declared them and the insurer accepted them.
  • Excess mileage, fair wear charges and damage charges on a contract hire agreement.
  • The motor excess, unless the GAP policy includes an excess contribution and your excess is inside that cap.
  • Racing, track use, and carrying paying passengers, where the policy excludes them.

A repair, and a finance balance that is not all eligible

Example only

£0 after a repair, then a £3,000 claim rather than £5,400

The repair estimate on a Nottingham car is £4,800. The market value is £15,000. The motor insurer authorises the repair. GAP pays nothing, because there is no total-loss settlement. The driver still has a car, and still has the finance agreement running as before.

A different car is a total loss. The motor insurer pays £14,200. The PCP settlement is £19,600, but £2,400 of that is negative equity brought across from the previous finance, and the GAP policy excludes carried-over negative equity. The claim limit is £10,000. The eligible shortfall is £19,600 minus £2,400 minus £14,200, which is £3,000. The claim is £3,000, not the headline £5,400. The limit would only have bitten if the eligible shortfall had been above £10,000.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

Read the exclusions against your own agreement

The exclusions that hurt are the ones that match your paperwork. A cash buyer does not care about an arrears clause. A PCP customer who rolled £2,000 of negative equity into the new agreement should care a great deal. The prescribed information a dealer must give you, under ICOBS 6A.1, includes the significant and unusual exclusions and a pointer to where they sit in the policy. If that page was missing, it is relevant both to whether you understood the cover and to a later complaint.

When a loss does qualify, the claim order is in how to make a GAP claim. When the car is repaired instead of written off, the categories are in the stolen or written-off guide.

Common questions

Does GAP cover my motor excess?
Only when the policy says so, and only up to the amount it states. A £450 excess is not automatically added to a GAP claim. If the wording contributes up to £250, the rest stays with you.
Are belongings in the car covered?
No. Phones, tools, child seats and luggage are a motor policy question, often with a low inner limit, or they are not covered at all. GAP is about the vehicle shortfall, not the contents.
Does GAP replace a warranty or breakdown cover?
No. Mechanical failure, servicing and a breakdown on the motorway are outside a GAP policy. If the car is repaired and handed back, there is no GAP payment.
If my claim limit is £10,000 and the gap is £12,000, do I receive £12,000?
No. You receive up to the claim limit. You still owe the rest. A limit below your realistic shortfall is a reason to reject the quote, not a detail to discover at claim time.
