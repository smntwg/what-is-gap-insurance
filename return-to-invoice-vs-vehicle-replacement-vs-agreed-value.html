Return to invoice vs vehicle replacement vs agreed value

Home / Guides / Return to invoice vs vehicle replacement vs agreed value

Return to invoice and vehicle replacement are types of GAP insurance. Agreed value is a way of setting the payout on your motor policy, not a GAP product. All three react to depreciation, but they use different figures, and the motor settlement is calculated first. You do not add the three payouts together.

Return to invoice

Return to invoice, often called RTI, pays the difference between the motor insurer's settlement and the price on your purchase invoice. It is the version closest to the ABI's description of GAP: the difference between the original purchase price and current market value. It suits someone who wants the money they paid for the car put back, whether or not they have finance.

The invoice definition matters. Factory-fitted options are often included. Dealer extras, paint protection and a warranty pack sometimes are not. A private sale receipt may not count as an invoice. If you financed the car, return to invoice can pay more than the finance balance, because the invoice is usually higher than the settlement figure once you have made payments and put down a deposit.

Vehicle replacement

Vehicle replacement GAP looks at the cost of a new equivalent at the time of the loss, not at the invoice you originally paid. If list prices have risen, the top-up is larger than return to invoice. If they have fallen, it can be smaller. Policies do not all use the same replacement price. Some say the manufacturer's list price. Some say the price of a comparable new vehicle. The definition is the cover.

This is also the cover most likely to duplicate a new-car replacement benefit on a comprehensive motor policy in the first year. Read that benefit before you pay for a second version of it.

Agreed value

Agreed value sits on the motor policy. You and the motor insurer agree a figure, often renewed each year, and that is what the motor policy aims to pay instead of arguing about market value after a total loss. It is more common on classic, imported and specialist cars, and on some motorbikes, than on an ordinary hatchback. If the agreed figure is kept up to date and the claim is accepted, the gap left for a GAP policy can be small or zero.

It is not a promise to pay the purchase price, and it is not finance cover. If you owe more than the agreed figure, you can still have a finance shortfall. If the agreed value is out of date, the motor insurer will follow the policy conditions, which may let it move back towards market value.

One car, three calculations

Example only

A £22,000 car, a £15,400 market value, and a £24,200 new equivalent

Sam pays £22,000 for a car from a dealer in Leeds. Eighteen months later it is a total loss. The motor insurer's market value is £15,400. A new equivalent is listed at £24,200. Sam has no finance.

If the motor policy pays the £15,400 market value, return to invoice tops up £6,600 to put Sam back to the £22,000 invoice. Vehicle replacement, if it pays up to the £24,200 list price, tops up £8,800.

If instead the motor policy is agreed value at £21,000, the motor insurer pays £21,000. Return to invoice would then top up £1,000, not £6,600. Sam is not paid the market-value gap as well. The agreed value has already changed the starting point.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

Do that before you accept a figure your GAP policy will then use as its starting point. The claim itself is covered in how to make a GAP claim.

Common questions

Is agreed value a type of GAP insurance?
No. Agreed value is an agreement with your motor insurer about what the vehicle is worth. If that policy pays, it pays instead of a market-value assessment. GAP, if you have it, is a separate contract that looks at what is left after the motor payout.
Can I buy return to invoice and vehicle replacement together?
You buy one GAP policy. The wording chooses the basis. Stacking two GAP policies on the same car usually fails a double-insurance condition. Read the proposal before you assume a second policy will pay.
What if a new equivalent now costs less than I paid?
A vehicle replacement policy that follows today's price of a new equivalent can pay less than return to invoice in that case. If you need the original invoice back, return to invoice is the closer match. If you need enough to order today's equivalent, look at the replacement definition in the wording.
Does comprehensive insurance already include new-car replacement?
Many policies do, for a limited period, often the first 12 months, and often only if you are the first registered keeper and under a mileage cap. That can overlap with vehicle replacement GAP during that period. It is a schedule benefit, not something every comprehensive policy includes.
