Is GAP insurance worth it?

Home / Guides / Is GAP insurance worth it?

It is worth buying when a write-off or theft would leave a bill you could not comfortably pay. It is a poor buy when that shortfall is small, you already have the savings, or your motor policy already replaces the car. GAP insurance is optional. It does not replace comprehensive cover.

Judge the shortfall, not the badge on the car

A comprehensive motor policy usually pays the car's market value at the time of a write-off or theft. The Association of British Insurers describes that payout, and describes GAP insurance as cover for the difference between the price you paid and that market value. If you have finance or a lease, the figure that matters may instead be what you still owe. GAP does not replace the motor policy. It can only top up a settlement the motor insurer has already agreed.

So the useful question is simple. If this car were written off next month, what would I still have to find, and could I find it without borrowing again?

When it is usually worth looking at

  • You bought a new or nearly new car and it is losing value faster than you are paying the finance down.
  • You are on a PCP and the settlement figure still includes a large optional final payment.
  • Your deposit was small, so you have little equity if the market value falls.
  • Your comprehensive policy has no new-car replacement benefit, or that benefit has already ended.
  • A bill of a few thousand pounds would mean more debt, not a dip into savings you already hold for this.

When it is usually not

  • You paid cash for an older car at a price close to what similar cars sell for.
  • You are well into a hire purchase agreement and the balance is already below a cautious market value.
  • You have savings earmarked for the difference and you would still have an emergency fund afterwards.
  • The claim limit is lower than the shortfall you are trying to cover. The policy cannot pay more than that limit.
  • You are being asked to buy finance GAP and you do not have finance. There is no finance balance for it to clear.

A worked example

Example only

A PCP shortfall of £4,650 against an example premium of £265

Maya buys a new hatchback in Birmingham for £26,490 on a PCP. She puts down £2,000. Sixteen months later the car is written off after a flood. Her motor insurer offers the market value, £17,800, and pays it to the finance company. The settlement figure that day is £22,450. The shortfall is £4,650.

In this illustration a three-year policy that covers the finance settlement costs £265, paid once. The example shortfall is much larger than the example premium. That does not mean Maya is likely to claim, and it does not mean £265 is a current market price. It shows why the cover can be rational for her, and pointless for someone who could pay £4,650 from savings without difficulty.

These pound figures are an illustration so you can see the arithmetic. They are not a quote, a market average, or a claim paid by a named insurer.

Price is part of the answer

The Financial Conduct Authority's evidence is that where you buy can change the price sharply. In its competition work it said add-on GAP sold with a car could be twice as expensive as broadly equivalent standalone cover. In February 2024 it went further on value: its data showed that only 6% of the amount customers paid in GAP premiums was paid out in claims, and it had seen firms paying out 70% of premium value in commission. Several insurers then paused sales until they could show a fairer deal.

Those figures are a reason to compare policies and to refuse a rushed sale. They are not a forecast that you personally have a 6% chance of claiming. Read the dealer and online comparison and the FCA rules on dealer GAP before you decide.

A sensible order of checks

  1. Ask the finance or lease company how a settlement figure would be built, including any balloon or early termination charge.
  2. Read your motor schedule for new-car replacement, agreed value, and the excess.
  3. Decide which figure you actually need covered: the invoice, the finance balance, the lease charge, or a new replacement.
  4. Compare at least one policy that is not sold in the same appointment as the car.
  5. Check the claim limit, the exclusions, and whether the car's age and mileage are allowed.

Common questions

Is GAP insurance required by law?
No. Motor insurance that covers your liability to other people is compulsory. GAP insurance is optional, and a firm selling it with a car has to tell you whether that contract is optional or compulsory.
Does a low premium mean the policy is good value?
No. A low price only helps if the policy would pay the shortfall you actually face. In its 2022 value measures data the FCA found that, across the GAP market, only 6% of premiums were paid out in claims. That is a warning about the product's market-wide value, not a prediction of your own claim.
Should I buy it if I can afford the shortfall?
Often no. If you have money set aside and a write-off would not push you into debt, keeping the risk yourself is a reasonable choice. Insurance is for a loss you do not want to fund from savings.
Does worth it mean I am likely to claim?
No. It means the cover matches a loss you would struggle to pay. Most policies are never claimed on. The FCA's market figures show claims were a small share of premiums, which is part of why the regulator challenged the value of the product.

Related guides

GAP insurance for PCP

On a PCP the settlement figure often still includes the balloon. That is the gap finance cover is aimed at.

GAP insurance for cash buyers

No finance means finance GAP has nothing to pay. Invoice or replacement cover is a separate question.

Dealer vs online GAP insurance

The same shortfall can be insured in the showroom or later online. The sale, and often the price, is different.

What GAP insurance does not cover

Repairs, refused motor claims, arrears and sums above the claim limit are the usual gaps in the cover.
